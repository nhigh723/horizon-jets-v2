Frequently asked questions

Horizon Jets is an air charter broker, not a direct air carrier. All flights are operated by licensed FAA Part 135 operators. The operator flying your trip is disclosed to you before you book.

Are you a charter broker?

Yes. Horizon Jets is an air charter broker. We arrange flights with licensed FAA Part 135 operators — the companies that own and fly the aircraft. We're not a direct air carrier and we don't operate aircraft ourselves. The operator flying your trip is disclosed to you before you book.

How much does it cost to charter a private jet?

It depends on the aircraft size and the length of the flight. Typical hourly ranges from published 2026 charter cost guides: a turboprop runs about $2,000–$4,000 per hour, a light jet $4,000–$6,500, a midsize jet $5,000–$8,000, a super-midsize jet $7,000–$10,500, a heavy jet $9,000–$15,000, and an ultra-long-range jet $12,000–$19,000 or more. Most one-way domestic trips cost roughly $7,000 to $65,000 for the whole aircraft.

Those are planning ranges, before the 7.5% federal excise tax, segment fees, landing charges, and positioning (the empty flight to get the aircraft to you). The only way to know your price is a quote for your exact trip.

How do I request a quote?

Fill in the form on our booking page with your departure and destination, dates, and passenger count. We source quotes from licensed operators for your route and dates and come back with real pricing — the aircraft, the operator, the schedule, and the full price broken down. There's no obligation to book.

Can I book a flight instantly online?

No — and be cautious of any site that promises that. Charter pricing depends on live aircraft availability, so every flight is quoted individually for your route and dates. The quote process is the booking process: once you accept a quote, we confirm the aircraft and arrange the booking.

Do you have your own aircraft?

No. Horizon Jets owns no aircraft and employs no pilots. All flights are operated by independent FAA Part 135 certificated operators. That's normal for a broker — our job is finding the right aircraft for your trip, not owning one.

Do you sell jet cards?

We don't run a jet card program. If you fly 25 or more hours a year, we can help you compare providers' cards — the rates, the terms, and whether a card actually saves you money at your flying volume.

What is an empty leg?

An empty leg is a repositioning flight — a jet flying empty to its next job — sold at a discount, often 25–75% below standard charter rates. The catch: the route, date, and rough departure time are fixed by the original booking, and the flight can be cancelled if that booking changes. Our empty-leg guide explains it in full. We don't list live empty legs, but ask us to check for one on your route when you request a quote.

How far in advance should I book?

As early as you can for the best choice of aircraft and price — a week or more is comfortable for most trips. That said, charter is built for flexibility, and flights are sometimes arranged with just a day or two of notice, depending on what's available. Last-minute requests are possible but the choice narrows.

What airports can private jets use?

Private aircraft can use more than 5,000 airports across the US — far more than the few hundred served by commercial airlines. That often means landing much closer to your destination. Smaller jets and turboprops can use even shorter runways that larger aircraft can't.

Are pets allowed?

Many operators allow pets in the cabin, but it depends on the operator and the aircraft — it's not universal. Mention your pet in the notes when you request a quote, and we'll match you with an operator that accommodates pets.

Is flying private safe?

Charter flights in the US are operated under FAA Part 135 regulations, which set standards for the operator, the aircraft, and the crew. Safety records and audit ratings vary by operator. When we quote your trip, we'll tell you which operator is flying it, and you can ask us anything about that operator before you book. Horizon Jets doesn't operate aircraft or hold safety ratings of its own — we're a broker, and the operator's record is what matters.

What does the quoted price include?

The hourly rate normally covers the aircraft, crew, fuel, and standard insurance. On top of that, a US domestic charter carries a 7.5% federal excise tax and a per-passenger segment fee ($5.30 per passenger per segment in 2026), plus landing and handling charges at each airport. Catering, de-icing, overnight crew expenses, and international fees may be extra depending on the trip. A proper quote itemizes all of it — if a quote doesn't, ask for the breakdown before you book.

Can I change or cancel after booking?

That depends on the operator's terms for your specific flight, which we'll explain before you commit. Cancellation terms vary widely — some bookings are fully refundable up to a point, others aren't. Always confirm the cancellation policy in writing before you pay anything.
