Empty leg guide

Empty leg flights, explained

An empty leg is the cheapest way to fly private — with the biggest catch in the industry. This page explains how they work, what they really cost, and when they're worth it.

Horizon Jets does not list live empty legs. We have no inventory feed and no live deals page. But when you request a quote, ask us to check for an empty leg on your route — we'll look at what's actually available for your dates and tell you straight whether one fits.

What is an empty leg?

When someone charters a jet one-way — say, New York to Miami — the aircraft usually has to fly back to its home base, or onward to its next booking. That return flight happens whether or not anyone is on board. The operator still pays for fuel, crew, and landing fees.

Rather than fly it empty, the operator sells that repositioning flight at a discount. That discounted flight is the empty leg. You get the same aircraft, the same crew, and the same private terminal experience as a full-price charter — you're just flying on a trip the jet was making anyway.

What do empty legs cost?

Brokers commonly advertise empty-leg discounts of 25–75% off the standard one-way charter price for the same route and aircraft. The discount depends on the route, the aircraft, and how urgently the operator needs to fill the flight — the deepest discounts usually appear in the last 24 to 72 hours before departure, when the alternative is flying completely empty.

There is no guaranteed discount. Treat the 25–75% range as a typical band, not a promise — and compare any empty-leg price against a standard quote for the same route before you commit.

The catches

The route and time are fixed

You're buying someone else's itinerary. The departure airport, destination, date, and rough departure time are set by the original booking. You can't change the destination, and usually not even the departure airport.

It can disappear

If the charter that created the empty leg changes or cancels, your leg goes with it. Empty-leg bookings carry real cancellation risk, and refund terms vary — check them before you pay.

The booking window is short

Many empty legs only appear a few days before departure, and some pop up the same day. That makes them hard to plan around if your dates are fixed.

You book the whole aircraft

Like any charter, you're paying for the aircraft, not a seat. One passenger or a full cabin, the price is the same.

Who empty legs suit

Empty legs suit travelers who are flexible on dates and airports, fly popular routes (New York–Florida, Los Angeles–Las Vegas, and similar corridors see the most empty legs), and can move quickly when a leg appears. They don't suit fixed schedules, must-make meetings, or anyone who needs a guaranteed flight.

How to ask about an empty leg

If your dates are flexible, mention it when you request a quote: give your route and a range of dates, and note that you'd like us to check for an empty leg. We'll look at what's actually available and tell you whether an empty leg fits your trip — or whether a standard charter is the better move.
